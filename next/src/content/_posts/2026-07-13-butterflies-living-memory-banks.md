What if butterflies are the world's living memory banks?

A caterpillar enters the chrysalis carrying everything its first body learned. Most of that life disappears, but perhaps the memories that matter refuse to die. They emerge folded into wings.

![A caterpillar resting near a translucent chrysalis hanging from a branch](/assets/images/butterflies-living-memory/caterpillar-chrysalis.jpg)

And when those wings begin to move, perhaps they do more than lift the butterfly. Perhaps every beat releases an old fragment into the world—a warning, a longing, an unfinished idea.

![A monarch butterfly moments after emerging from its chrysalis, wings still folded and wet](/assets/images/butterflies-living-memory/butterfly-emerging.jpg)

Maybe that is why inspiration sometimes feels less like invention and more like remembering something we never lived.

![A macro close-up of iridescent butterfly wing scales — intricate patterns suggesting memory, movement, and the passage of time](/assets/images/butterflies-living-memory/wing-macro.jpg)

What if a butterfly passing near us is not simply travelling?

What if it is returning a memory to the world?
